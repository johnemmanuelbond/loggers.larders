### Intro Scene
> A slow scraping rumble signals the opening of a trapdoor from beneath a pile of broken masonry and shattered wood. A tattered purple tapestry moves aside as three figures ascend from beneath the ruins of Thorngarten. Velchar, Vauric, and Vorandel emerge with distraught faces into a scene of carnage and destruction. The monastery is leveled. The stone walls are caved in, shelves of priceless tomes and ancient artifacts lay in piles of broken rubble. Only the venerable bones of the lodge remain standing, draped in steaming viscera as they are. The corpses of mutated fish-like creatures slowly drip into puddles of dark blood which litter the ground across the whole mount and throughout the surrounding clearing in the forest. Suspended, barely, by metal wires is a mangled bronze device, still reflecting a beam of multicolored light down at a stone pillar on the perimeter of the monastery. The distant cracking sound is all that signals the slow awakening of this skinsplinter horror, the last of twelve, as bone white and dark red colors return to its limbs and it slinks back into the forest. Silence reigns.
> 
>Vauric: "They have destroyed it!"
>Velchar: "We can rebuild. Did you hear? The warden is come again! We need only keep the valley safe for a while longer."
>Vorandel: *despondantly* "How could we? We've lost it all."
>Vauric: *Walking over to one of the mutated corpses*  "The warden's own weapon... come to punish us. Letting the outsiders in was a mistake. If they hadn't come--"
>Velchar: "No Vauric, this was coming with or without them. I had been trying to warn you, we could never just ignore the shaay-luuq forever."
>Vauric: "And now the skinsplinter have risen. No druids have brought us news in fifty winters."
>Velchar: "One of the outsiders had the gift, and maybe the villagers keep contact with their kin. There is still hope."
>Vorandel: *snapping out of it* "The village! They must be warned. There are some there whose blood carries our power."
>
>The three elven monks set about descending hastily the sloped path from the monastery down the rocky mount to the clearing in the forest. They pass a grizzly scene of half-eaten horses whose would-be devourers are plastered against the stable floors, covered in cauterized slashes. They quickly dart across, out of breath when the reach the forest line. Entering the wood they raise their hoods and slow their steps. A grim triad of dark-robed figures hugging the long shadows of the ancient trees, nary a leaf rustles as they proceed. What they do not see in those shadows is the wisp of a hand, and a pair white eyes following them, waiting until the ragged Vorandel lags behind the others... before snapping out at his neck.
>
> Vorandel is lifted five feet in the air as a shadow peels off a tree to make contact with his throat. The hunched form of a ten foot tall hag with a crown of glowing stars and jagged wooden limbs bursting from her back glares up at Vorandel and speaks with disgust.
> Grendelle: Hello again, High Abbot. I was wondering when you vermin would emerge, I have need of you."
> Vorandel: "You... Despoiler. I defy you!"
> Yelling "Fly you fools!", Vorandel breaks free of the hag's grasp with a practiced maneuver and blasts a squall of frost point blank into Grendelle. The hag barely flinches but her attention is drawn. Vauric protests but Velchar grabs him by the shoulder and the two elves mutter a prayer, fading into faint blue outlines and fleeing westward. 
> Vorandel doesn't put up much of a fight. In less than a minute he is bleeding and burnt, strung up by his wrists on a wall of thorny vines as skinsplinters hold him. Grendelle closes in, her claws like razors. Under a rain of vicious slashes the High Abbot Vorandel is no more, his blood shining white with power as Grendelle pierces one of the skinsplinters with her hand. It shrieks as it withers and the hag grows ever taller, ever brighter, ever darker.

To the chorus of you and multitudious other disembodied spirits, burning away one by one, they take this power into themselves. All according to plan.
